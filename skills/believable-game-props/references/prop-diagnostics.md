## Sofa, the primary case

**Construction evidence.** FoamOrder documents wrap that rounds foam edges and can produce a crown. Sailrite shows top/bottom panels and a continuous boxing strip, separate from an alternative that omits boxing. Cushion.com distinguishes its boxed and waterfall/knife-edge constructions. IKEA's KIVIK gallery supplies a photographed example, dimensions and a cross-section showing the relation of cushions and support. These suggest which construction to model. They do not prescribe numerical loft, seam position, or contact for another sofa.

**Modeling heuristics**

1. Lock the room placement, frame footprint and intended cushion volumes. Trace the deck, back support, seams, and negative spaces in a matching photo. Compare scaled front, three-quarter, and section views. Distinguish a loose boxed seat from a wrap-front design and a firmer seat from a softer back.
2. Block the cushion as a volume. Use a clean mesh with controls for the perimeter, broad crown and section profile. Set the maximum loft across a broad area, roll smoothly where the top turns down over the front, and transition into a side wall or seam according to the reference. Keep the underside comparatively firm where it bears on the deck. A bevel around a flat box changes edges but does not create a crown. Judge the untextured volume under a broad light and in profile.
3. Form the seam along the boundaries of sewn cover panels. A boxed cover may have a top-to-boxing seam with optional welt, whereas a wrapped surface can carry the seam off the exposed front. Do not add piping to every edge. Use a slim geometric welt only where it changes the silhouette or separates forms at play distance. Bake subtle seam creases or stitches if visible under raking light. Avoid a dark outline in albedo as a substitute for construction.
4. Inspect cross-sections at the front, sides, and rear. Bring the actual bearing surfaces into believable contact at the deck or back support. Slightly flatten the hidden underside or locally compress a side/back where constrained, keeping the silhouette of the free face. Preserve separations between neighboring loose cushions, arm clearance, and any designed front overhang. If sections show correct support, check shadows in the target light before changing the mesh. Do not glue gaps shut, intersect exposed faces, paint in fake occlusion, or sag every cushion. Add asymmetry only if the reference and art direction call for it.
5. Gate surface work on the gray views. At the normal player view, look for a readable top/face transition and clear support, not isolated stitches. At the nearest view, verify sewing paths, texture scale and first readable relief. Compare before/after with fixed position, lighting and camera.

## Other construction decisions

- **Seating.** Identify upholstered components, rigid brackets/frame and legs. Match the swatch. Fine leather grain and angular reflections vary by leather finish. Woven fabric usually needs correctly scaled weave and softer grazing response, not enlarged leather pores. A powder-coated frame is not the same finish as bare polished aluminum. For actual quilting, make stitch lines recess shallowly as padded cells rise. Bake relief when the perimeter holds, and use geometry only when the quilt changes profile or visible depth. If the pattern is meant to be printed, keep it flat.
- **Electronics.** Use one keyboard ref in the right locale and size. Check full-size versus TKL, ISO versus ANSI Enter, stagger, modifiers and space bar. Choose geometry for readable gaps/keycap heights and a texture for legible legends. Prefer no tiny legends to shimmering pseudo-text. On screens, separate the active surface from bezel and shell. Read power state before treating darkness as a defect. Use finish-specific reflections, softened for matte surfaces, and no fake room image baked into the panel unless explicitly agreed as a target fallback. Rear ports and seams do not outrank front shape and response.
- **Cabinetry and drinkware.** Establish solid wood, real veneer, laminate, or other intent before UV work. Align face grain with each board, rail, stile or panel. Use end grain only for actual exposed solid-wood ends. Intentional veneer matching can carry continuity across panels. A glass rim and base affect silhouette and light differently from a ceramic cup or opaque bottle cap. Prefer differences in proportions and response over gratuitous transparent layers or labels.
- **Billiards.** Felt-like cloth can cover the rubber nose, so the construction break may read in section and highlight rather than as a separate exposed rubber color. Give balls normal sphere shading and a controlled highlight consistent with a finished resin surface and the actual light. A readable shaft taper, tip and butt usually do the job for the cue.
